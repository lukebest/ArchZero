# 2609.00857: LLM Inference on IMC-NoC Architecture with Balanced Dataflow and Fine-Grained Parallelism

- arXiv: [2609.00857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00857) · PDF: [arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00857](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00857)
- Venue: journal-style extension of the authors’ ICCAD 2025 LEAP paper (self-cite [6], p.2); cs.AR preprint 1 Sep 2026. 11 PDF pages. Not a new lab.
- Domain tag: LLM decode / NoC
- One-line hardware fact: a mesh of macros that pair a 128×128 8-bit RRAM IMC PE with a router that holds scratchpad + 16 MACs; collectives are compile-time spanning-trees/rings; LEAP-D physically drops IMC from a decode partition and grows its scratchpad.
- Lab note: Yimin Wang, Yue Jiet Chong, Xuanyao Fong — same NUS ECE group as CHIPSMORE ([2608.30509](2608.30509.md)). CHIPSMORE’s spatial mapping is this paper’s (CHIPSMORE p.6, [29]). This paper is the intra-die fabric and PD-disaggregation study; CHIPSMORE is the chiplet / LoRA / multi-request sequel. Do not score them as independent inventions.

## 四视角独立批评

### 微架构

LEAP is a *typed* mesh: each matrix class is sentenced to a different physical unit.

**Structures.** A macro = IMC PE + router. The router *is* the NMC+INC: scratchpad, matrix-multiply logic (IRCU), interconnect, reduction (p.3–4). IMC PE: 128×128, 8-bit cells, area/power taken from Peng et al. 2019 [25] (Table IV, Table V). Router: 256 B buffers, 64-bit packets, 16 MACs, 16-bit scratchpad datapath. LEAP-A: every macro has IMC + 32 KB scratch. LEAP-D: prefill macros look like LEAP-A; decode macros *delete the IMC PE* and grow scratch to 64 KB so the decode region is a scratchpad bandwidth farm (Fig. 2(d) p.2; Table IV p.7). That deletion is the only structural move this extension adds over a homogeneous IMC mesh.

Hierarchy they name: tile / 4 channels per tile / 32 RPUs per channel / 8 macros per RPU; 64 tiles for Llama 3.2-1B, scaled up for larger models (Table IV). An RPU is “a single row of macros within a channel”; an RG is the RPUs that own one column-partition of W_Q/K/V or one row-partition of W_O (p.4–5). Inter-layer placement is a *serpentine* so the last decoder’s token can walk to the first without a long hop (Fig. 5, p.4).

Data types (their coinage): DSMM = dynamic × static (IMC); DDMM = dynamic × dynamic (IRCU); reduction of partials (INC in the router) (p.2–3). Collectives are a closed set (Table II, p.5): Broadcast (spanning tree), Reduce (reversed tree, IRCU), ReduceScatter / E-ReduceScatter / AllReduce (ring or decomposed tree, IRCU), AllGather (pipelined with MAC), MAC (1 iteration in IMC; scratchpad-depth in NMC). All of this is *deterministic at compile time* (p.5). No adaptive routing, no virtual channels discussed, no credit scheme beyond “rout. buf. 256 B.”

Mapping heuristics, not hardware: same-matrix partitions occupy a contiguous *rectangle*; row- or column-major unroll; attention occupies `2⌈D/C⌉ × 2⌈D/C⌉` macros; Q/K/V column-major inter-tile, O row-major; V transposed intra-tile only (p.4). KV is *cyclic append* across scratchpads in the mapped region so decode growth load-balances local memory (Fig. 4, p.4). Prefill dataflow is a named sequence Broadcast-1 → MAC-1 → ReduceScatter-1 / elementwise-ReduceScatter-1 → AllGather-1 → MAC-2 → … (Fig. 6–7, p.5–6). Decode in LEAP-A *degrades* ReduceScatter-1 to Reduce-1 because there is one token (p.6). LEAP-D prefill overlaps KV shipment to the decode region with AllGather-1/2 and claims zero critical-path tax (p.6, Fig. 8).

Batching (B=2 only): decode of A and prefill of B share the *same* IMC weights in the prefill region; they stall one or the other at layer granularity depending on whether A needs weights during B’s attention or during B’s projection loops (Fig. 9, p.7). That is an ad-hoc mutex, not a hardware scheduler.

**Mechanism vs policy.** Mechanism: typed macro (IMC vs. fat-scratch router), deterministic collective primitives, serpentine layer walk, LEAP-D’s IMC-less decode macros, cyclic KV placement. Policy: rectangular mapping heuristics, PD split ratio (never given as a number of tiles), B=2 stall rules, 64-bit / 16-MAC design point they later say sits on a roofline (Fig. 13, p.9).

**Reverse-engineer objections.** 128×128 analog RRAM is a 2019 circuits paper, not their array. IRCU “16 MACs” is a multiplier count, not a reduction tree diagram. “Balanced dataflow” is the claim that trees/rings plus overlap keep the mesh from hot-spotting — they never show per-link occupancy, only a critical-path instruction mix (Fig. 12, p.9) in which IMC almost never appears. That last fact is damning for a paper titled around IMC: the critical path is IRCU mul/add and movement. IMC is a weight ROM with a multiply.

### 负载

The workload is Llama 3.2-1B / Llama 3-8B / Llama 2-13B, single-request sweeps of input/output length, plus a *two-request* random-arrival random-length batch (p.7, p.9). End-to-end Table VI uses a fixed 1024+1024 window (p.8). They say B=2 “remains sufficient for most edge applications” because scratchpad capacity and the disaggregated layout cannot do more (p.7). That sentence is the paper confessing its serving envelope.

What actually stresses LEAP: the 4–6× decode-vs-prefill throughput hole in LEAP-A, blamed on underfilled pipeline stages when one token walks the mesh (p.8, Fig. 7(c)). LEAP-D’s broadcast of the current token across the decode region is the intended fix (p.8). Batching improves TTFT and hurts TPOT because the two requests mutex the IMC (Fig. 15, p.9). That is a real tradeoff, and they are honest that batching is not universally better (p.9).

What is missing, especially next to the sequel. No LoRA (CHIPSMORE’s job). No multi-request beyond 2. No MoE. No context at 4k–8k (CHIPSMORE’s S/L/XL). No quality. No continuous batching. No decode-only long generation (the 1024+1024 mix hides how bad TPOT is if the user asked for 8k tokens). Models stop at 13B; “tile # scaled accordingly” (p.7) is not a 70B experiment. Prefill-decode *aggregation vs. disaggregation* is compared to DistServe/Sarathi/Adrenaline in Table III (p.7) as checkmarks — they keep attention disaggregated and projections/FFN aggregated “to attain the IMC resource limit” (p.7), i.e. they would not pay for a second IMC copy. That is a capacity constraint dressed as a serving philosophy.

Baseline fairness: A100 / H100 tokens/s and tokens/J at 1.4 / 1.7 GHz vs. their 1 GHz modeled fabric (Table VI, p.8). 8B LEAP-A 202 tokens/s vs. A100 78 vs. H100 274; LEAP-D 446 vs. H100 274; energy 19–21 tokens/J vs. H100 0.78. Same CIM-vs-GPU category error as CHIPSMORE, at smaller models and without the H100 350 W row — they report efficiency not power. Still no description of the GPU software stack (vLLM? eager HF?).

A workload reviewer will also note the scaling essay (p.8–9): model size ~ `s_e²` and `s_e s_h` in parameters, critical path only `s_e s_l` or `s_h s_l` because collectives follow the longest row/column. That is a *mesh-collective* claim. It is only as good as “the critical path is the longest route,” which their own Fig. 12 says is IRCU/movement, not IMC. Fine. It does not survive irregular MoE expert maps or non-rectangular mappings.

### 仿真方法学

Digital IRCU+control: Verilog, DC on *FreePDK 45 nm* [32], Innovus P&R, PrimeTime with post-synthesis SAIF (p.7). Then “digital results are obtained on 45 nm PDK and then scaled to 7 nm” (Table V footnote, p.8). IMC PE: adopted from [25], 32.37 µW and 0.0864 mm², 72% of a LEAP-A macro area (Table V). Scratchpad: CACTI (p.7). Performance: “instruction-level accurate simulator customized based on the NoC instruction set” (p.7); batching is *on top of* layer-wise and operation-level time estimates (p.7). That last clause means B=2 is not a full-system contention simulation.

So: 45→7 nm scaling of the energy hog (router is 56% of macro energy, 17.5% of area, p.8), a 2019 IMC macro, CACTI SRAMs, and an ISA simulator that already assumes the deterministic schedules. Overlap of KV transfer with AllGather is *declared* free (p.6). Fig. 13 sweeps packet width × IRCU parallelism and they pick 64-bit / 16-way as “near-optimal” (p.9) — that sweep is the most honest tools work in the paper, and it is still a parametric model of the same ISA.

What was not measured: NoC congestion under non-ideal mapping; analog error; 45 nm layout vs. 7 nm wires; GPU measurement methodology; any model larger than 13B; any batch >2; refresh (no eDRAM here — KV is SRAM only, so LEAP *cannot* do CHIPSMORE’s XL without the sequel’s third tier). Area of the 8B/13B *system* is not given; they show a 2×2 macro layout (Fig. 10, p.8).

Do not treat this simulator and CHIPSMORE’s simulator as two labs confirming each other. CHIPSMORE says it implements “the IPCN instruction set along with the proposed mapping and dataflow schemes [18], [29]” (CHIPSMORE p.7). Same group, same ISA, same mapping.

### 工业可行性

LEAP-A already wants, for 1B: 64 tiles × 4 channels × 32 RPUs × 8 macros = 65,536 macros, each with a 128×128 RRAM (Table IV). Even if those numbers are hierarchical and not a flat 64k, the paper is talking about *thousands* of analog crossbars plus a mesh. Yield, test, programming time, and conductance drift are absent. 45 nm P&R of a 2×2 then “scale to 7 nm” is not a tapeout plan (p.7–8).

LEAP-D’s industrial tell: they *cannot* put a second weight copy on the decode side, so decode macros have no IMC (p.4, p.6). That is honesty about RRAM capacity. It also means decode Q/K/V *projections* still have to go back to the prefill region’s IMC (Broadcast-1, MAC-1, Reduce-1 “remain the same,” p.6) — disaggregation is incomplete. Attention DDMMs move; weight DSMMs do not. Table III’s checkmark for “operation-level disaggregation” is therefore not DistServe. Adrenaline disaggregates projections/FFN and keeps attention together; they do the opposite “to attain the IMC resource limit” (p.7). A silicon team would hear: we only had one weight image.

Software: compile-time collectives and a custom mapping DSE. No serving stack. B=2 mutex rules (p.7) are a research scheduler. Scratch 32–64 KB/macro × tens of thousands of macros is a lot of SRAM the CACTI line-item never sums.

What breaks: analog IMC as a trusted 8-bit ROM; 45→7 nm energy for the router that is already 56% of power; serpentine placement when you add or remove a layer; cyclic KV when context exceeds the decode-region scratch (they do not say the max context at 64 KB); any request rate that wants B>2; any LoRA (no SRAM-CIM in *this* paper). The sequel adds those boxes because this design does not have them.

## Master Class

1. **The Real Abstract** — An NUS IMC-mesh template (ICCAD 2025, now extended) that runs DSMMs in 128×128 RRAM, DDMMs and reductions in the router, and compile-time trees/rings for the rest. LEAP-A is a homogeneous mesh; LEAP-D deletes IMC from a decode partition, doubles that partition’s scratch, and overlaps KV shipment with prefill collectives. Evaluated in an ISA simulator on Llama 1B/8B/13B at 1k+1k, batch 1–2, vs. A100/H100 tokens/s and tokens/J.

2. **The Rashomon Synthesis** — Microarch and industry agree the *structure* is “IMC as weight ROM, router as the real processor” (Fig. 12, Table V) — then split: microarch likes the typed collectives; industry sees 45 nm scaled to 7 nm and a 2019 crossbar. Workload and simulation agree LEAP-A’s decode hole is the paper, and LEAP-D is a bandwidth patch — then split on whether Table VI vs. H100 is evidence or unit mismatch. Against CHIPSMORE: this paper’s workload person is *more* honest about B=2 and batching hurting TPOT (p.9); CHIPSMORE’s workload person will say LEAP never left the edge envelope. The core tension: the title is IMC-NoC; the critical path is not IMC (Fig. 12).

3. **The Magic Trick** — Give every matrix type a home (static→IMC, dynamic→scratch+IRCU, partials→in-router reduce) and, when decode starves, physically grow the scratch farm and broadcast one token to all of it.

4. **The Skeleton in the Closet** — Extension of their own ICCAD paper (p.2). Digital energy from 45 nm, scaled to 7 nm (Table V). IMC from Peng 2019 (p.7). Batch 2 is a capacity confession (p.7). GPU comparison without a measured GPU setup (Table VI). KV transfer “has no time overhead due to the overlap” (p.6) — by construction in the ISA model. No accuracy. Same group as CHIPSMORE; not a second confirmation.

5. **可提取的问题线索**

- Fig. 12 shows IMC rarely on the critical path; if the mesh+IRCU dominate latency, what problem is the analog 128×128 PE actually solving besides weight storage? (p.8–9, Table V)
- LEAP-D decode macros have no IMC, so Q/K/V projections still execute in the prefill region — how much of “PD disaggregation” is attention-only, and what is the cross-region trip cost when overlap fails? (p.6, Fig. 8, Table III)
- Cyclic KV append balances scratchpads only inside a mapped rectangle; what happens when decode context exceeds that rectangle’s 64 KB×N capacity? (p.4, Table IV)
- Deterministic spanning-tree/ring collectives assume rectangular maps; which real layer shapes (GQA, MoE, sliding window) break the “all dataflow is Table II” claim? (Table II p.5; p.4)
- LEAP-A decode is 4–6× slower than prefill because one token underfills the AllGather/MAC pipeline — at what token width (speculative N, small batch) does that hole close without LEAP-D? (p.8, Fig. 7)
- B=2 batching mutexes IMC between decode-A and prefill-B (Fig. 9); what is the TTFT/TPOT Pareto when arrivals are bursty rather than the paper’s two-request story? (p.7, Fig. 15)
- Critical-path scaling is claimed as `s_e s_l` not `s_e s_h s_l` (p.8–9) — for which collective primitive is the longest row/column actually the bound, and when is it the IRCU MAC depth instead? (Fig. 12–13)
- 64-bit packets × 16-way IRCU is picked as a roofline point (Fig. 13) — what happens to that point when KV traffic (not just attention DDMM) is on the same links? (p.6, p.9)
- Digital power is 45 nm P&R scaled to 7 nm while IMC is a 2019 macro — what is the 7 nm router vs. IMC energy if both are drawn in one PDK? (Table V, p.7)
- Table VI vs. A100/H100 at 1024+1024 — what are the GPU batch, precision, and kernel stack, and is tokens/J counting the same tokens? (p.8)
- Serpentine inter-layer placement (Fig. 5) — what is the hop-count tax when layer count does not match the floorplan’s fold? (p.4)
- They compare PD checkboxes to DistServe/Sarathi/Adrenaline (Table III) but keep one IMC image — what serving SLO (TTFT vs. TPOT vs. goodput) is this opposite split even aiming at? (p.7)
- Scratchpads in prefill K/V channels are “mostly idle” outside the transfer window (p.10) — what fraction of LEAP-D’s doubled decode SRAM is the paper’s real area bill for the throughput in Fig. 14? (Table V, Fig. 14)
- Instruction-level simulation plus “batching modeled on top of layer-wise estimates” (p.7) — which contention (IMC mutex, mesh links, scratch banks) is therefore not in the B=2 numbers? (p.7, Fig. 9)
